==========================================================
SCALING LAWS (Kaplan et al. / Henighan et al. 2020)
==========================================================

General form:

  L(X) = (X_c / X) ^ alpha_X

Where:
  L     = test loss
  X     = the variable being scaled (compute, data, or parameters)
  X_c   = a constant (fitted from experiments)
  alpha = the scaling exponent

Exponents (from LLM research, used as approximation):
  alpha_C = 0.05   (compute)
  alpha_D = 0.095  (dataset size)
  alpha_N = 0.076  (parameters)


To find the multiplier needed to halve the loss:

  L_new / L_current = 0.5
  0.5 = (X_current / X_new) ^ alpha
  X_new / X_current = 2 ^ (1 / alpha)

==========================================================
KEY TAKEAWAY
==========================================================
The small exponents mean halving the loss requires
enormous scaling in any single dimension. This is the
diminishing returns property of the power law.

In practice, optimal performance requires scaling all
three factors (compute, data, parameters) in tandem,
not just one in isolation.

NOTE: These exponents were derived for LLMs. Your
actual model may scale differently, but the general
trend of diminishing returns holds across architectures.
